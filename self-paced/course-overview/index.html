Where it started: the Ralph loop

while :; do cat PROMPT.md | claude-code done

Wake up · read the plan · do the next step · tick it off · go back to sleep ↺

⏱ 00:02–00:03 · 1 min
🎤 Aldric
  • Geoffrey Huntley's post “Ralph Wiggum as a ‘software engineer’” (July 2025, ghuntley.com/ralph): in its purest form, a Ralph loop is a Bash loop that starts a coding agent again and again with the same prompt.
  • It's named after Ralph Wiggum from The Simpsons: well-meaning, but maybe not all that bright. Each time round, a new agent session wakes up, looks at the plan, does the next step, ticks it off and goes back to sleep. No single session has to think very hard.
  • Matt told this in September's session 1, at 02:12:22. Use his telling as a guide.
  • “A Ralph loop is the smallest software factory there is. The factory you'll build grows from it.” Hand to David.

  • “This is the same Ralph loop, now as an assembly line in a factory: a planner writes and updates the plan, and ralph does each step.”
  • Then the three words, which the rest of the course uses. A factory is software that holds one or more assembly lines and runs them. An assembly line is machines in an order, fixed by whoever designed it. A machine is an agent that's handed its inputs and runs to completion, with no person in the conversation.
  • “In your first hour, you run this yourself, in your own copy of the starter project.”

  • Slack: #cohort-build-a-software-factory-self-paced. “Ask whenever you're stuck. An instructor replies within 1 business day, and other students and alumni often answer sooner.”
  • The times follow the Zoom meeting, which is set in Pacific time. Eastern is always 3 hours later. The UK is 8 hours later, except for about a week in late October and two or three weeks in March, when the US and UK change their clocks on different dates and it's 16:30 UK. That's why the slide says UK rather than BST or GMT.
  • Lean Coffee: a 60-minute call; everyone posts questions on the meeting's Zoom Whiteboard, votes, and we discuss the most popular. If you can't come live, post your question in Slack first and we'll put it on the board. Questions that aren't discussed still get an answer in Slack.
  • Feedback: an instructor replies to your three capstone ideas within 2 working days, then gives feedback on your vision and your first iteration plan, in Slack.
