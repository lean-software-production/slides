Framing: audience, outcomes, concepts, logistics

Audience

  • Experienced engineers who have just paid $1,950 and enrolled on their own, on any day. Nobody else starts with them.
  • They already use a coding agent. Most have read the landing page; few have seen a factory run.
  • They can ask for a full refund within 14 days if they have completed less than 25% of the course, so this video is the strongest reason they have to keep going.
  • July's students who left early said they couldn't see where the course was going until a factory was shown in session 6. In September, the most convincing moments (capstone show and tells, Relay, Memba, the closing round) were all in the last two sessions.

Learning outcomes

  • Say what a software factory does, from having watched one build Tetris.
  • Name one thing an earlier student built, or learned, with their factory.
  • Say how the course works: build part of the factory, then use it on your capstone, coached by your own coding agent.
  • Know where to get help (Slack and the weekly Lean Coffee) and what to do next.

Key concepts

Format & logistics

  • Slides for the presenters to talk over, with clips cut in during editing. The slides mark where each clip goes.
  • Clips come from the video library. Student clips can only be used once each student has agreed: see testimonial-candidates.md. We decided on 2026-10-25 to ask September students for permission. David asks each student once the clips are chosen.
  • Jeremy presents Relay in the session 8 clip but isn't on camera here; name him, and check he's happy for it to be used.
  • Slides: the deck, 10 slides; the speaker for each is in schedule.tsv. Times are whole minutes from the start of the video, so short slides (the opening line, who we are, the next step) show as a few seconds.
  • No dates, days of the week, "this cohort" or "next session" anywhere. A student may watch it a year from now.
