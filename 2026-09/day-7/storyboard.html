Framing: audience, outcomes, concepts, logistics

Audience

  • Eleven active students; Farid watches the recordings.
  • Four show their capstone today: Ed Snodgrass, Rob Westgeest and Matt Penning signed up on Maven, and Joel Howe on the Miro board. Fouad and Marek chose Thursday. Each was asked to prepare: what did you build, how did you build it, and what did you learn?
  • Weekend homework, due today: iteration 005a (machine configuration), 006 (watching), bonus 007 (steering), and more capstone iterations, using a strong model to read a run's events.jsonl and improve a machine's AGENTS.md. They were asked to tell us what problems they found and which machine improvements helped.
  • On Thursday, under What's missing?, they asked for:
    1. A reasonable factory design from us: three or four things that worked and three or four we had to change, and why (Marek; Joel asked in chat for examples of good factory harnesses).
    2. How to test the stochastic parts of a factory (evals).
    3. Loops, and how to limit them (raised in office hours).
    4. Theory of constraints and value stream maps (Matt; Jeremy could show the value stream map in Relay).
  • Several said the homework took their whole weekend. This is the last weekend homework.

Learning outcomes

  • Describe what watching (and, optionally, steering) a run showed them, and one change they made because of it.
  • Say what a classmate built, how, and one thing they learned from it.
  • Describe the foreman pattern: what it does before a line runs and after it finishes.
  • Describe the superpowers workflow, and say which part of it they would bring into their own factory.

Key concepts

  • Foreman: an agent in a dedicated conversation that helps a person shape work for a software factory, invoke its assembly lines, and monitor progress. When a line finishes, the foreman helps them recover the original intent, consider whether they asked for the right thing, and understand the changes well enough to guide the next work. (lexicon)
  • Superpowers: a skills library for coding agents that turns one conversation into a disciplined workflow: brainstorm a design, write a plan, then execute it task by task with subagents, test-first, with review between tasks.

Format & logistics

  • Zoom for talking; the cohort Miro board for the work.
  • Before class, on Miro: a How did the weekend go? frame with What I saw and What I changed columns; the Show and tell slide frames from Thursday, in order Ed, Rob, Matt Penning, Joel; a parking area for questions that need longer; prepare a space for takeaways and remaining puzzles.
  • Message Ed, Rob, Matt Penning and Joel the day before: order, about 6 minutes each, screen share or Miro frame, and that a recording of their factory running is a fine fallback.
  • David: a prepared foreman conversation (one line running, one finished) and a recorded fallback. Three minutes is short: show one moment, not the whole loop.
  • Aldric: slides for superpowers, no live demo.
  • Matt: Memba, its factory, and a recorded fallback.
  • A Thursday requests board with dots, built after the reflection.
