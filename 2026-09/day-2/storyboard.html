Framing: audience, outcomes, concepts, logistics

Audience

  • 12 working developers and tech leads; they met each other on Tuesday.
  • Before today they could: build their own coding agent, run the Tetris Ralph loop (or the Fabro one), watch "Software Factory: a working definition", write three capstone ideas. Some did all of it, some none.
  • Some are advanced and worried the course won't go deep enough.
  • At stake: the weekend. They leave with a capstone and know how to start the tutorial, or they drift.

Learning outcomes

  • Can explain, with a drawing, one of: how a coding agent works under the hood, the pros and cons of a Ralph loop, what a software factory is.
  • Have named what surprised them, what clicked, what still puzzles them and what they wish for, and heard the others'.
  • Have committed to one capstone idea, written in the shared Google Doc.
  • Know what to do for homework and what's coming next.

Key concepts

  • Coding agent: a loop around a stateless model: the conversation grows each turn, and tools let it act.
  • Ralph loop: a shell loop running an agent against a plan. What's awesome about it, what sucks.
  • Software factory: our working definition from the video: a loop, made of parts.
  • Not today: validation, machines, assembly lines (Day 3).

Format & logistics

  • Zoom for talking and breakouts; the Zoom whiteboard for all the working (More → Whiteboards; people in breakouts open it themselves). Not changing tools this week.
  • The backward-facing half is a W³ (the homework, teach back, reflections): the instructions are spoken and the work happens on the whiteboard. Those slides are just a title, no instructions.
  • Drawings are Sharpie, not pencil: rough on purpose, something to point at while you talk.
  • Instructors aren't in the prep rooms; they're on call, and correct during the teach-back.
  • David is the planned Zoom co-host and operates both breakout rounds. Confirm his co-host access in the Day 2 meeting before starting. Plan for three rooms. With 12 students, that is roughly four per room; actual teach-back groups depend on students' choice of subject.
  • Run-of-show assignments for instructor confirmation: Matt keeps overall session time; David watches the breakout timer and closes each round; Jeremy watches chat and leads the Zoom whiteboard segments. David posts the capstone sheet in chat before the second round. The presenter notes name the speakers for each handoff.
  • Backup for breakout controls: confirm Matt has host or co-host controls before the session. If David cannot operate the rooms, Matt takes over while Jeremy gives the spoken instructions. If neither has controls, keep everyone in the main room and prepare the three topics together; do not wait for breakouts to work.
