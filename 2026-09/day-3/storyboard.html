Day 3: Validation, and your capstone (Tue 6 Oct)

Third live session of cohort 2. 09:30 to 11:00 PT, 90 minutes, on Zoom. Led by Matt and Jeremy. Syllabus source of truth: the "Cohort 2 (Sept/Oct 2026) Syllabus Draft" Google Doc. This file is the facilitator's plan for the session, not student-facing.

Objective: over the weekend, students got their hands on a validation loop: tutorial iterations 001 and 002, the same Tetris seed built twice, the second time with a validator looking through a lens. We lay out what happened and how the tutorial felt, make sense of what validation did, then turn to riding the bicycle: they watch us write a capstone's vision and plan its first iteration, and their homework is to do the same for their own capstone and run their factory on it. Assembly lines move to Thursday.

They arrive having built a factory that checks its own work, mostly alone. They leave having said how it felt, pooled what they know about validation and heard what we think is true, and having watched a vision and a first plan get written, ready to do their own.

Framing: audience, outcomes, concepts, logistics

Audience

  • 12 working developers and tech leads, in their second week; each has picked a capstone in the shared sheet (Robinson hadn't yet; Jeremy is chasing him).
  • Before today they could: do tutorial iterations 001 and 002, read the HTML comparison of tetris-001 and tetris-002 their agent built for them, watch the Doer vs Validator and Justin McCarthy videos.
  • Some will have done all of it and some none of it: plan the class for both.
  • At stake: Thursday. They leave with a vision for their capstone, or they stay stuck building bikes and never ride one.

Learning outcomes

  • Have said what they did, how the tutorial felt and what they learned from it, and heard everyone else's.
  • Can say what difference validation made in their own build, and why "none" is a fine answer.
  • Have pooled what the cohort knows about validation, and taught some of it back.
  • Have seen how an agent helps write a vision and plan iteration 1 from it, and know their homework, including which context engineering reading they signed up for.

Key concepts

  • Validation is the mold: without it the agent is a slop cannon spraying molten plastic (McCarthy); with it, every copy comes out alike.
  • Lens: what the validator looks for. Same seed, different lens, different code.
  • Vision: a short document of what the capstone is for and who it serves; each iteration's plan refers to it.
  • Not today: machines, assembly lines, routing; deep research and knowledge bases (a bonus). Context engineering is Thursday's teach-back.

Format & logistics

  • Zoom for talking and breakouts; the working happens on the cohort's Miro board ("Building Software Factories, September cohort"). Day 1's hopes and fears go on it as a picture.
  • Two breakout rounds, both random rooms made by Zoom and run by Aldric: pairs or threes for the tutorial retro (1-3-All, one round instead of two); three groups for the teach-back. Rooms open and people join when they're ready.
  • Jeremy does all the screen sharing, so the windows don't jump about when the presenter changes.
  • Feedback as questions, so other students get to answer first.
  • Before the session: Matt runs the capstone demo once (vision, "interview me" for plan 001, run the factory), so there's a prepared session; the Saturday clip "Writing a vision and planning the first iteration" (4:43) is the fallback.
