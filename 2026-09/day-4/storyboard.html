Framing: audience, outcomes, concepts, logistics

Audience

  • Twelve developers and tech leads. On Tuesday they watched instructors plan the first iteration of a capstone, then were asked to try it with their own projects.
  • They may have written a vision, planned an iteration, run their factory on their project, and researched context engineering. Some will have done only part of this.
  • They have used a factory that plans, does, and validates work. They have not yet built or edited an assembly-line graph; iteration 003 comes after this session.
  • Running a factory on a real project may have shown them what it needs next. This session helps them choose a change to try.

Learning outcomes

  • Describe what their factory did on their capstone, including useful work, stops, and moments when they had to step in.
  • Teach back what they learned from their context-engineering research and suggest how a factory could use it.
  • Use something they saw in that run to discuss a change to the factory, which may involve better context, validation, or another kind of work.
  • Explain that iteration 003 lets them edit the route as a graph, and iteration 004 lets a target hold more than one line. Name one change to try on their project.

Key concepts

  • Machine: a non-interactive agent or deterministic step that receives an input and returns a result.
  • Assembly line: a directed graph of machines whose connections determine what runs next.
  • Orchestrator: the part of the factory that runs the line, handles branches and retries, and decides when it finishes.
  • Context for a machine: the information it needs for its own work, selected from what the team knows about the project.
  • Project-specific validation: a check that helps the factory decide whether this project's work is good enough to continue.
