Framing: audience, outcomes, concepts, logistics

Audience

  • 12 working developers and tech leads who already use a coding agent every day.
  • Walk in excited and a bit sceptical: "agents are powerful but they run amok, can this be tamed?"
  • Some did the pre-work (coding agent harness, Tetris Ralph loop). Some got stuck on setup. Some haven't started.
  • At stake: whether the next four weeks feel worth the evenings of homework.

Learning outcomes

  • Can say, in a sentence, what they want to be able to do by 22 Oct.
  • Can describe what they saw the Fabro factory do, in their own words.
  • Can name the rhythm of the course: short homework Tue to Thu, big homework Thu to Tue, talk about it live.
  • Leave with a first candidate idea for their capstone.

Key concepts

  • Software factory: a system that turns a seed into working software with little human help. Just the gist today; the video in the homework does the rest.
  • Ralph loop: the simplest factory. A shell loop running an agent against a plan.
  • Capstone: the factory you build over the course, and the thing it builds. Something you'd like to exist in the world, ambitious enough to be daunting.
  • Inverted classroom: you do the work at home, we make sense of it together live.

Format & logistics

  • Zoom for presenting, talking and breakouts. No EnsembleWorks for students today.
  • 12 students, breakout rooms of 2.
  • Speakers: Matt, Jeremy, Aldric and David; who speaks each slide is in the speaker notes.
  • A pre-recorded Fabro run in case the live one fails.
