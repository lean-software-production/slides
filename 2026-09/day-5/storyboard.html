A machine, up close

See the reading take shape in one concrete machine

Talk, two slides: a plan conformance checker's harness and context, with Böckeler's guides and sensors labelled, then the same machine as the Miro card students fill in next

Notes
  • The worked example is a plan conformance checker: a machine that compares the codebase with an implementation plan, reports matches, gaps and differences, and judges the work on rigor, fidelity, quality and releasability.
  • Its harness: read access to the plan and the codebase; access to the defect tracker (GitHub Issues); read access to an API that asks another system for a screenshot of a page on the website; POST access to one endpoint, where it submits its results. Nothing else.
  • Its context: an AGENTS.md that gives it the task and the four criteria, and tells it how to find the plan to evaluate. Everything else it reads on demand.
  • Label the AGENTS.md as a guide. The screenshots and defect tracker are how it observes the software. The machine itself is an inferential sensor for the line: a model's judgement on the doer's work. It can't see the doer's conversation, so it judges the work, not the story. The second slide is the same machine as a Miro card, the one students fill in next.

Framing: audience, outcomes, concepts, logistics

Audience

  • About twelve developers and tech leads, starting week 3. Over the weekend they were asked to do Session 4's homework: tutorial iterations 3 and 4, with bonuses to customize their assembly line and add a second one; run at least one capstone iteration through the factory; read Birgitta Böckeler on harness engineering and share three key ideas.
  • Maven promised them a harness-engineering teach-back today; instead they put the reading to work on machines for their own capstones. Session 4 showed the reading often isn't done, so a worked example comes first.
  • They have an editable assembly line and can choose between lines. They have not yet seen a line that runs machines at the same time.

Learning outcomes

  • Describe a change they made, or want to make, to their assembly line, and what happened when they ran it.
  • Define the harness and context of at least one machine for their capstone's assembly line: what steers it before it acts, what checks its work, and what it is given and not given.
  • Name something they wish their assembly line could do, and say whether it belongs in a machine, a check or the route.
  • Explain how a line fans out to several machines and fans back in, using the three big brains as the example, and know the homework.

Key concepts

  • Harness: everything around the model call. Böckeler splits the part we build into guides, which steer the agent before it acts, and sensors, which check its work afterwards; each can be computational (a script) or inferential (a model's judgement).
  • Fan out and fan in: a machine with several unlabelled edges out runs every machine they lead to at the same time; the branches meet at one machine, which runs once all have finished and is given their answers.
  • The three big brains: the same review run on three providers' models, with a synthesiser that decides. It is the tutorial's example of fan out, not a requirement of it.

Format & logistics

  • Zoom for talking and breakouts; the cohort Miro board for the work.
  • David is the Zoom host and runs every breakout, including its timer and broadcasts. He also gives the demo; no room block overlaps it.
  • Before class, on Miro: an Our assembly lines frame with What I changed and What happened columns; a Design your machines frame per group, with one card for the group, titled with the machine's name, with the eight boxes from slide 8: job, allowed, not allowed, result, context given, left out, guides, sensors; a What would you want a line to do? frame. Reuse the retro and takeaways frame.
  • David: prepare a factory with iteration 005 done and a line using the three big brains: one run in progress, one finished, and a recorded fallback. The tutorial gives specifications, not an implementation, so it has to be an instructor-built factory.
  • Add the homework to Maven as a new section for Session 5, and publish it before the session.
