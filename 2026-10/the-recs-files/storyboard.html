Total: 45 minutes: 30 of talk, 15 of questions. Any overrun comes out of the questions. People recognise four problems from their own codebases, see the records that one project keeps, then take the problems one at a time: what the recs files do so each stops building up. They close by watching the agent stop at a disagreement and ask, and a decision recorded live and agreed by the team. The student-facing agenda has six parts: Two code paths, The words drift, Comments invent reasons, Context grows, Decisions go back to the team and Try it Monday. The problem slide and the record diagram come before the agenda: the first names the problems, the second gives the vocabulary for the fixes.

Framing: audience, outcomes, concepts, logistics

Audience

  • Developers who already use a coding agent. Most have never seen the recs files.
  • They have lived with the problems this lesson names, even if they've never put them into words.
  • They take in little at a time: one idea a slide, and no full records on screen.

Learning outcomes

  • Name five ways agentic development gets worse over time, and trace each one to a decision nobody wrote down.
  • Say what the recs files do about each: plans and code cite the record they realize; one word per record, swept by stem; a comment is a citation; the agent reads titles first and opens only what bears; a question is framed from the records.
  • Explain why the agent stops and asks the team when two records, or a plan and a record, disagree.
  • Install the recs files and start a project's records with recs-kickstart.

Key concepts

  • Record: a short, numbered statement of one decision, in a file of its own, cited by number and title.
  • Projection: the index file for one category, listing every standing or proposed record by number and title. The agent reads the projections to see which records exist.
  • Ubiquitous language: one record per word, saying what it means and nothing about behaviour. The agent speaks in those words.
  • Citation: the only kind of comment in code: a record's number, section and title.
  • Record keeper: the team. The agent proposes; the team agrees.

Format & logistics

  • Zoom, one room, no breakouts, starting 12:00. Answers in chat. Recorded and posted publicly by Maven.
  • One toy project throughout: the plot register (a community garden), from the repo's eval fixture planning-without-a-realization-modality, extended in live-fixture/ with three architectural decisions (ADR-1 superseded by ADR-2) and one vision sentence for the live decision to make untrue.
  • Live track: one run at the end, in a fresh copy of live-fixture/: Aldric records the web-app decision from live-fixture/prompt.md, then agrees to it. The answers are in that slide's notes. The fixture is ours, not one of the repo's evals: dry-run it.
  • Code on the problem slides (Gardener.get_plot, Plot.allocate_to_gardener, the two comments, the Elixir clauses) is illustrative; none of it is in the fixture.
